In a real game

Go by the smallest ship left

The spacing comes from the shortest ship your opponent still has. While the destroyer is afloat, play the checkerboard. Once it sinks, widen to every third diagonal, and keep widening as the smaller ships go down. If a 2-cell and a 5-cell ship are both left, the 2 decides.

Hunt, then target

The pattern finds ships. It doesn't sink them. After a hit, leave the pattern and fire at the four squares around it. Once a second hit shows the direction, keep firing along that line until the ship sinks, then go back to the pattern.

Fire the middle first

Inside the pattern, start near the center and work outward. More ship positions pass through the middle of the board than along its edges, so central shots find ships sooner on average.

Why diagonals never miss

Label every square with (row + column) mod 3, as in the corner of the board shown here. Any 3 squares in a line, across or down, contain one 0, one 1 and one 2. Fire on every square labeled 1 and a 3-cell ship can't avoid all of them.

The same holds for any length: with mod 4, any 4 squares in a line hold each label once. Pick the label with the fewest squares and that's the pattern.
